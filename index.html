Abstract

Pictography, among the earliest known writing systems, conveys meaning through symbolic visual structures that represent objects, actions, and abstract concepts. While prior research has largely focused on static pictography recognition and completion, the absence of dynamic and interpretable representations limits public understanding and engagement. In this work, we aim to generate semantically correct and structurally stable animations for pictographic glyphs.
Directly applying existing Image-to-Video (I2V), sketch and clip-art animation models or typography animation methods to pictographic glyphs remains challenging due to their bias toward realism, structural instability, and limited support for fine-grained, part-wise motion control. To address these challenges, we propose LivingGlyphs, a unified end-to-end framework that transforms pictographic SVGs into expressive and coherent animations. At the core of our framework is an MLLM-based Pictographic Planner augmented with a pictography vector knowledge base, which directly operates on SVG representations to generate structured semantic descriptions, part-wise motion semantics, and coarse trajectory priors.
To ensure stable and interpretable motion control, the generated trajectories are further parameterized and smoothed via a continuous Bézier-to-B-spline process, yielding temporally coherent simple motion priors. For structural control, we extract skeleton representations for each glyph part and initialize temporally aligned skeleton keyframes. During animation generation, we adopt a differentiable T2V optimization framework that refines motions under semantic guidance, while enforcing geometry-aware regularization through skeleton length and conformal angle constraints. The final animated shapes are reconstructed using As-Rigid-As-Possible (ARAP) deformation, preserving the structural stability, readability, and symbolic integrity of pictographic text. Extensive experiments demonstrate that LivingGlyphs produces high-quality, living pictographic animations with stable multi-part motions, highlighting its potential for cultural revitalization and educational applications.
